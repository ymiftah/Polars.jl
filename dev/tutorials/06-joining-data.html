Joining Data ​

innerjoin combines rows from two frames that match on one or more key expressions, keeping only rows with a match on both sides — exactly like a SQL INNER JOIN.

Joining a fact table to a dimension table ​

Bring store names into the orders table:

julia
orders_with_stores = innerjoin(orders, stores, col("store_id"))
head(orders_with_stores, 5)
5×8 DataFrame
 order_id  timestamp            store_id  product_id  quantity  unit_price  st ⋯
 Int64     Dates.DateTime       Int64     Int64       Int64     Float64     St ⋯
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        1  2024-01-01T00:25:00         2           4         2         3.2   R ⋯
        2  2024-01-01T02:09:00         2           5         2         3.0   R
        3  2024-01-01T02:13:00         2           6         2         4.2   R
        4  2024-01-01T02:57:00         1           4         4         3.2
        5  2024-01-01T04:27:00         3           4         3         3.2     ⋯
                                                               2 columns omitted

Since both tables have a column named store_id, a single shared key expression is enough. When the join columns are named differently on each side, pass one expression per side instead: innerjoin(a, b, col("id_a"), col("id_b")).

Joining in a larger pipeline ​

Joins compose with the rest of the lazy API. Revenue per store name (rather than raw store_id), continuing straight from the join:

julia
revenue_by_store_name = @chain orders begin
    lazy
    with_columns((col("quantity") * col("unit_price")) |> alias("revenue"))
    innerjoin(lazy(stores), col("store_id"))
    group_by("store_name")
    agg(sum(col("revenue")) |> alias("total_revenue"))
    sort(col("total_revenue"); rev = true)
    collect
end
3×2 DataFrame
 store_name  total_revenue 
 String      Float64       
───────────────────────────
  Riverside         1620.4
     Uptown        1447.85
   Downtown         1335.2

Joining against multiple dimension tables ​

Bring in both store and product details at once by joining twice:

julia
enriched = @chain orders begin
    lazy
    innerjoin(lazy(stores), col("store_id"))
    innerjoin(lazy(products), col("product_id"))
    select(col("order_id"), col("store_name"), col("product_name"), col("category"), col("quantity"), col("unit_price"))
    collect
end
head(enriched, 5)
5×6 DataFrame

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Keeping unmatched rows: leftjoin ​

innerjoin drops any row with no match on the other side. leftjoin keeps every row from the left frame instead, filling missing where the right side has no match — the join to reach for when you want to enrich a table without silently losing rows that don't have a lookup match. To see it in action, join orders against a deliberately incomplete stores table (only two of the three stores):

julia
partial_stores = filter(stores, col("store_id") .<= 2)
with_missing_stores = leftjoin(orders, partial_stores, col("store_id"))
select(with_missing_stores, col("order_id"), col("store_id"), col("store_name")) |> x -> head(x, 8)
8×3 DataFrame
 order_id  store_id  store_name 
 Int64     Int64     String?    
────────────────────────────────
        1         2   Riverside
        2         2   Riverside
        3         2   Riverside
        4         1    Downtown
        5         3     missing 
        6         1    Downtown
        7         1    Downtown
        8         1    Downtown

Orders from the missing store (store_id 3) keep their row, with store_name as missing, instead of disappearing the way they would with innerjoin.

The rest of the join family ​

leftjoin/innerjoin cover the two most common cases, but Polars.jl has the full SQL join vocabulary — rightjoin, outerjoin, semijoin, antijoin, crossjoin — plus join_asof for matching on the nearest key rather than an exact one (covered in Time-Series Analytics). They all share innerjoin's calling convention (one shared key expression, or one per side). See the Joins reference section for the full table and join_asof's strategy options.
